# Research Notes: Cache Eviction Strategy Comparison

## Overview

We evaluated three eviction strategies for the Kestrel-7 caching layer: **Adaptive LFU**, **Windowed LRU**, and **Hybrid Score-Based Eviction (HSBE)**. Each was benchmarked against a synthetic workload of 2.4M requests generated by the Thornbury trace simulator.

---

## Approach 1: Adaptive LFU

Adaptive LFU tracks access frequency with exponential decay. The score for an item $i$ at time $t$ is updated as:

$$
S_i(t) = S_i(t_{prev}) \cdot e^{-\lambda (t - t_{prev})} + 1
$$

where $\lambda$ controls decay sensitivity. In our trials, $\lambda = 0.015$ gave the best hit ratio (91.2%).

```python
def update_score(prev_score, delta_t, decay=0.015):
    return prev_score * math.exp(-decay * delta_t) + 1
```

> [!note]
> Adaptive LFU performed best under skewed access patterns (Zipf $\alpha > 1.2$) but degraded under uniform access.

## Approach 2: Windowed LRU

Windowed LRU restricts recency tracking to a sliding window of size $w$. This reduced metadata overhead by roughly 18% compared to standard LRU, at a small cost to hit ratio (87.6%).

> [!warning]
> Window size below 500 entries caused thrashing during burst traffic in the Halvorsen dataset.

## Approach 3: Hybrid Score-Based Eviction (HSBE)

HSBE combines frequency and recency into a single weighted score:

- Frequency component
    - Normalized decay score
        - Adjustable half-life parameter
            - Tuned per shard
- Recency component
    - Last-access timestamp delta
