# By hand: WP 2.5, more than one window

2026-09-09. Against the installed release build — `pnpm tauri build
--bundles app`, copied to `/Applications/MD Reader.app` — driven with
real keystrokes and a real mouse (`CGEvent` press, drag, release), with
screenshots of the desktop rather than of one window, because what is
being checked is what several windows do to each other.

## The setup

Two folders under one root: `wp25/` with `brief.md` and `plan.md`, and
`wp25/notes/` with `one.md` and `two.md`. The first window opened
`wp25`; the second opened `notes`. Both windows were resized to 700×500
and put side by side, so that a drop point is unambiguously over one
window or over none.

## What the run found

**The webview cannot say where a drag ended.** The first implementation
sent `dragend`'s `screenX`/`screenY` to Rust and let it hit-test the
windows with them. Dropping a tab at (300, 700) made a window at
(347, 716); dropping the next at (250, 900) made one at (320, 880) —
out by (47, 16) and (70, −20), in different directions. A drop within
seventy pixels of a window's edge would have gone to the wrong window,
and a torn window never lands where the reader let go.

Rust reads the pointer itself now, through the window manager, in the
units window frames are already in. Re-checked after the change: a tab
dropped at (1200, 200) made a window whose corner is at (1200, 200).

**An event addressed to one window was arriving at all of them.** Found
by reading Tauri's event matching rather than by running anything: a
listener registered the ordinary way asks for events to any target, and
every emit reaches those, addressed or not. The fix is that each window
listens as itself. Check 12 is what proves it: with five windows open,
clicking the close button of one closed one. Before the fix, the
before-close event would have reached all five, and each would have
answered by closing itself.

**A tab dropped onto a window did not bring it forward.** It does now:
the reader dropped it where they were looking.

## Moving a tab, in detail

`brief.md` was dragged from the left window's strip into the middle of
the right window. The left window's status bar read *Moved brief.md to
the window under it* and its strip was left with `plan.md`; the right
window drew the document, kept its own `notes` tree in the sidebar, and
said *brief.md moved here*.

In the right window the document was put into Edit mode and a sentence
typed at the end. The tab took the dirty dot, the status bar said
*Unsaved changes*, and autosave wrote it. Then the tab was dragged back
to the left window: it arrived in **Edit**, with the typed sentence, at
19 words.

Cmd+Z there took the sentence away again — an undo, in the window that
did not make the edit, of an edit made in the window it came from. Twelve
words, *Saved*, and `brief.md` on disk back to what it held before.

## Tearing, and the two ways to do it

Dragging `one.md` out of the `notes` window and letting go over the
desktop made a window at that point holding `one.md` alone, with no
sidebar — a new window is empty of everything but the tab it was made
for.

`Move Tab to New Window` from the command palette made a window offset
28 points down and right from the one the tab came from, the same size,
in front. No pointer is involved in that one, which is the point of
having it.

## Windows, closed and remembered

Five windows were open. The close button of one closed that one and left
four. Closing another and then quitting with Cmd+Q left three in
`session.json` — the three that were open, each with its own frame, its
tabs and its folder. The relaunch put back exactly those three: the
`wp25` tree in one, the `notes` tree in another, `brief.md` in the third.

With the window holding `brief.md` in the background and a folder window
in front, `open -a "MD Reader" brief.md` brought the window holding it
forward and opened nothing anywhere else. That is the WP 1.8 rule with
more than one window to choose from, which until now it never had.
